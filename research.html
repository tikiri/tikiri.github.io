Research

Sea level rise, hydrologic extremes under nonstationarity, Everglades modeling, climate and water resources, and machine learning.

Sea level rise & coastal flooding

Developing sea level rise scenarios and probabilistic projections of extreme water levels, and using them to assess flood risk to communities and infrastructure. This includes co-authoring NOAA’s U.S. sea level rise scenarios (2012, 2017, 2022) and the Department of Defense regional scenarios, and studying compound flooding where storm surge, high tides, rainfall and groundwater interact in South Florida’s low, porous landscape.

Selected work: Sweet et al. (2022, 2017) NOAA sea level rise scenarios; Ghanbari et al. (2019, 2020, 2021) on nonstationary coastal flood frequency and compound flooding; Jane et al. (2020); Peña et al. (2023).

Hydrologic extremes under nonstationarity

Classical engineering design assumes that floods and droughts follow the same statistics year after year. With climate and land use changing, that assumption no longer holds. This work, much of it with Jose Salas, re-derives return period, risk and design-flood uncertainty for nonstationary conditions and reviews methods for assessing water infrastructure.

Selected work: Salas & Obeysekera (2014, ASCE Norman Medal); Obeysekera & Salas (2014, 2016); Salas, Obeysekera & Vogel (2018); Obeysekera & Salas (2020).

Everglades restoration & hydrologic modeling

Regional hydrologic and hydrodynamic models were the technical backbone of the Kissimmee River Restoration and the Comprehensive Everglades Restoration Plan. This work covers model development, pre-drainage hydrology of the Everglades, operational control in integrated models, and evaluating how the Greater Everglades would respond to future climate.

Selected work: Landscapes and Hydrology of the Pre-drainage Everglades (2011); Obeysekera et al. (2011, 2015); Park, Obeysekera & VanZee (2005, 2007).

Climate change & Florida’s water resources

Trends in Florida temperature, rainfall and evapotranspiration; how well climate models represent Florida’s hydrology; downscaled rainfall projections; future droughts; and what these mean for water supply and flood protection.

Selected work: Florida’s Climate: Changes, Variations & Impacts (2017, co-editor); Irizarry-Ortiz et al. (2013, 2025); Haider et al. (2026).

Machine learning for floods & groundwater

Deep learning models for flood prediction and forecast-informed flood mitigation in South Florida’s canal system, machine learning prediction of groundwater levels, and reviews of machine learning for climate downscaling.

Selected work: Shi et al. (2023, 2024); Rogers et al. (2026); Najafi et al. (2026).

Sri Lanka

Long-standing collaboration with Sri Lankan universities and agencies on rainfall and temperature trends, water scarcity, design rainfall for the Colombo Metropolitan Region and training of engineers. After the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, he was co-principal investigator on an NSF-funded study of tsunami impacts on groundwater, soils and vegetation in coastal Sri Lanka.
